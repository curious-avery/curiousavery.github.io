Selected work

Projects

2026—PresentEnvironmental microbiology

Plastic-associated microbiomes in urban runoff

Independent research in the planning stage. The study is designed to use high-throughput sequencing to characterize microbiomes associated with plastic materials in Ballona Creek and compare them with control substrates.

2026—PresentClinical AI benchmark

DermLatBench

Co-developed a benchmark testing whether vision-language models can map visible anatomy to patient-centered left–right laterality. Work included curation and annotation of wound and dermatology images and co-authorship of the SAWC Fall 2026 poster.

SAWC poster

2026—PresentHuman benchmark

LeftRightQuest.org

Co-developed a public-facing laterality task in which users classify patient-side orientation from clinical images. Human responses can be compared with model performance.

LeftRightQuest.org

2026—PresentHealth information engineering

Carenery

Developed a privacy-conscious, browser-based health information tool with source-linked condition guides, public ClinicalTrials.gov and CMS NPI searches, and local appointment-question sheets. Favorites and drafts stay in the browser; the app does not diagnose or prescribe.

2025—2026Field ecology

Microplastics in California coastal mussels

Planned and conducted a survey across more than 25 urban creeks, jetties, and piers from Imperial Beach to San Simeon to examine runoff-associated hotspots and establish baseline data for future polymer and microbiome work.

2026MIDL Short Papers

Laterality Failure in Vision-Language Models on Surface Clinical Photographs: A Pilot Safety Study

Mitchell H. Liu, Avery H. Liu, and Minghsun Liu.

Open-access paper

Fall 2026SAWC

DermLatBench: Repurposing Wound and Dermatology Image Databases for AI Laterality Benchmarking

Avery H. Liu, H. Suzuki, S. Suzuki, and Mitchell H. Liu. Conference poster presentation.

Conference poster

DermLatBench dataset summary: 12,541 annotated clinical images; 9,770 images (77.9%) with sufficient visible anatomy for body-region or landmark classification; greater than 95% multi-annotator agreement; baseline laterality accuracy of 28% and 46% for two tested vision-language models.
